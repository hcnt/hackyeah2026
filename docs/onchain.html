OnSight · what happens on Solana

Every account, every transaction, every lamport

One program, on_sight, with three kinds of account and five instructions. Below: what each account stores, who pays for it, and a full event replayed transaction by transaction with exact amounts.

The numbers come from running the compiled program in LiteSVM, not from estimates. 1 SOL = 1,000,000,000 lamports. Every signature on a transaction costs 5,000 lamports.

Basics

Three Solana rules everything follows from

  1. 1
    A program can't remember anything by itself. Everything it remembers lives in accounts, small data records on chain. Our program owns three kinds.
  2. 2
    Every account must hold a deposit ("rent"), sized by how many bytes it stores: (128 + bytes) × 6,960 lamports. Whoever creates the account puts the deposit in. When the account is closed, the deposit goes to whoever the program's close rule names.
  3. 3
    Our accounts have computed addresses (PDAs). Each address is derived from fixed words plus keys, for example ["sighting", event, attendee]. Anyone can compute where an account lives without asking us, and the program can find the same address again on every call.

There is no settings account and no platform treasury: the fee (0.002 SOL per paid attendee) is a constant in the program, copied into each Event when it's created, and paid to the oracle whose report triggers the payout. Attendees own no account in our program. Their wallet is a normal Solana wallet that only receives the reward.

The fee is how running an oracle pays: whoever runs one (we do, as OnSight) covers the transactions and deposits and earns 0.002 SOL per attendee its report pays. With several oracles on one event, only the oracle whose report triggers the payout earns it; the others spent their report fees for nothing on that attendee.

The decision

Exactly what report_sighting checks, in order

Any failed check cancels the whole transaction: no account is created, no deposit is taken, and no SOL moves except the 10,000 fee the oracle paid to send it.

  1. before
    Solana runs Ed25519SigVerify. A wrong signature fails the transaction here, before our program runs.
  2. accounts
    Anchor checks the accounts: the Instructions sysvar is the real one, and the Sighting is at ["sighting", event, attendee] (created here if it doesn't exist yet).
  3. 1
    The signer is one of the event's oracles, else NotOracle.
  4. 2
    The instruction right before is the signature check of the attendee's wallet, over a message starting with Attend Now / Action: join / Event: <this event> / Wallet: <this attendee>, else BadJoinProof.
  5. 3
    start ≤ now ≤ end, else NotStarted or Ended.
  6. 4
    Already paid? Then stop with success and change nothing.
  7. 5
    Update the Sighting: first report or a gap over 60 s restarts first_seen and clears the oracle bits. Then set last_seen = now and this oracle's bit.
  8. 6
    Not enough different oracles yet, or seen for less than min_seen_secs? Stop with success; only the Sighting changed.
  9. 7
    paid_count < max_paid, else CapReached.
  10. pay
    paid_count += 1 and paid = true first, then Event −(reward + fee), attendee +reward, the reporting oracle +fee, and the AttendeePaid log line.

A paying report uses 22,656 compute units, far under the default limit of 200,000.

There is no admin and no settings to change. No instruction pays on someone's request, and nobody (us included) can take an event's budget while it runs. Changing the fee would need an upgrade of the program, which only its deployer can do, and running events keep the fee they were created with.

Fine print

Details that matter in practice

  • Failed reports still cost the 10,000 fee, for example a report sent before the start (NotStarted).
  • The reward must be at least 890,880 lamports (~0.00089 SOL) when the attendee's wallet is empty. A brand-new wallet must receive at least that minimum deposit, or Solana refuses the transfer.
  • Reading is free. The widget, stage and oracle read Event, Sighting and OracleInfo through RPC without signing anything. The widget shows the event's name, reward, times and spots left straight from the Event account, so an oracle can't misstate the terms.
  • Everything on chain is public: event terms, oracle keys and URLs, and for each attendee seen their wallet, the times, which oracles reported, the payout, and their signed join message (inside the report transaction).
  • Never on chain: photos, face data, names, and the wallets of people who joined but weren't seen.

OnSight · end-to-end flow · Solana devnet

From a locked budget to a paid attendee, step by step

An organizer locks a reward budget in a Solana program. Attendees opt in with their wallet and one selfie. At the venue a phone camera streams to face-recognition oracles. The oracles only report what they see. The program decides who gets paid, and pays each attendee once.

Who is involved

Four parties and one program

Organizer or sponsorCreates the event, chooses the oracles and the rules, and locks the budget. Runs the stage screen at the venue.
AttendeeHas a MetaMask Solana wallet. Joins through the widget on the organizer's page with one selfie and one signature.
Oracles (1 to 3)Face-recognition services, like ours (OnSight). Each holds its own key and reports sightings to the program. They can't move money; the program pays an oracle 0.002 SOL when its report triggers a payout.
Camera phoneAny phone at the pay lane or kiosk. Opens a link from a QR code and streams frames to every oracle.
on_sight programHolds the budget, stores the rules, and makes every payout decision on the chain clock.
0

One-time setup

deployer and oracles
  1. on chain
    Deploy the program to devnet. There are no settings to initialise: the fee (0.002 SOL per paid attendee) is a constant in the program, and there is no admin.
  2. on chain
    Each oracle registers itself with register_oracle(name, url), for example OnSight at https://hackyeah.kindhome.io. This creates a public directory entry, so a widget can find each oracle's API from the chain instead of trusting our server for it. The entry has no effect on payouts.
1

Organizer creates the event

organizer's wallet
  1. on chain
    create_event(event_id, oracles, threshold, start, end, reward, max_paid, min_seen_secs, name, venue) creates an Event account. Its address is the event's ID everywhere else (widget, oracle API, stage).
  2. SOL moves
    The whole budget, max_paid × (reward + fee), moves from the organizer into the Event account (escrow). From now on the organizer can't take it back while the event runs.
  3. on chain
    The rules are frozen: which 1 to 3 oracle keys may report, how many different oracles must agree (threshold, M-of-N), the time window, the reward, the fee, the cap, and how long a person must be seen (min_seen_secs, measured on the chain clock).

Today this is scripts/devnet_event.py. An organizer UI is optional later work.

2

Attendee joins (before or at the event)

widget on the organizer's page → every oracle
  1. read
    The widget <attend-now-widget event-id=…> reads the Event from the chain (name, venue, reward, times, spots left, oracle keys, threshold) and shows those terms itself, then reads each oracle's registry entry to get its URL. From the oracles it only takes the consent text and the "going" count.
  2. off chain
    The attendee connects MetaMask (Solana) and takes one selfie. POST attendance/test checks quality (one face, frontal, sharp, lit, big enough) and gives instant hints. It goes to one oracle only: the first of the event's oracles to answer when the widget loads, which also supplies the consent text. It's only a convenience: on join every oracle checks the photo again. Nothing is stored at this step.
  3. off chain
    They accept the consent and sign one message with their wallet. It's a message signature, not a transaction, so it costs nothing and needs no SOL:
    Attend Now Action: join Event: <event address> Wallet: <attendee wallet> Consent: 2026-10-03 Time: 2026-10-03T12:00:00Z
  4. off chain
    The widget sends the same signed join and selfie to every oracle of the event. Each oracle checks the signature (valid, under 5 minutes old, never used before), that it is listed on the event, the consent, the photo quality, and that this face isn't already registered under another wallet.
  5. off chain
    Each oracle keeps a 512-number face embedding, AES-GCM-encrypted under a per-event key that exists only in memory. The selfie itself is discarded. It also keeps the signed join message, which it will need for its reports.
  6. off chain
    The widget shows success once at least threshold oracles accepted the join.
Nothing reaches the chain at this step. Someone who joins and never shows up leaves no public trace.
3

Venue setup: stage screen and camera phone

organizer → oracles → phone
  1. off chain
    The organizer opens stage.html#<event> and signs one camera-token request with the organizer wallet. The page sends it to every oracle. Each oracle checks that the signer is the event's organizer and returns a camera token and a stage token.
  2. off chain
    The stage shows a QR code. The link is camera.html#…, with every oracle's URL and camera token in the URL fragment. The fragment never reaches any server.
  3. off chain
    The phone at the pay lane scans the code, opens the camera, and streams JPEG frames over one WebSocket per oracle (/api/v1/camera/{token}). Each oracle sets its own pace with immediate acks (up to 10 fps).
  4. off chain
    The stage connects to every oracle's live feed (/events/{id}/live) and shows the video with face boxes, a short wallet label (7xKX…gAsU), and stats.
4

Recognition inside each oracle

every oracle, independently
frame→ detect faces (SCRFD)→ embed (ArcFace)→ match against the decrypted guest list→ track + 3-of-5 vote→ wallet identified

Video relay and recognition are separate, so the stage video stays smooth even when recognition takes about 1 s per frame on the CPU server. Only people on the guest list can ever be matched. Anyone else gets an unlabeled box and is never reported.

5

The oracle reports a sighting, and the program decides

oracle key → on_sight

While a wallet stays identified, the oracle sends a report every 2 seconds until one comes back paid. Each report is one transaction with two instructions:

Transaction (fee payer and signer: oracle key) ├─ #0 Ed25519SigVerify native Solana program │ pubkey = attendee wallet │ message = the signed "Attend Now / Action: join / Event / Wallet …" from step 2 │ signature= the attendee's signature from step 2 └─ #1 on_sight.report_sighting accounts: oracle, event, sighting PDA ["sighting", event, attendee], attendee, system program, Instructions sysvar

If the signature in #0 is invalid, Solana rejects the whole transaction. Then the program runs these checks, in order:

1 · Listed oracleThe signer is one of the event's oracles (NotOracle).
2 · Attendee's consentThe previous instruction is an ed25519 check of the attendee's key over a join for this event and wallet (BadJoinProof).
3 · Inside the windowstart ≤ now ≤ end on the chain clock.
4 · Record the sightingThe first report creates the Sighting account (the oracle pays its rent). A gap of more than 60 s restarts the dwell time.
5 · Enough oraclesThe number of different oracles that reported (a bitmask) is at least threshold.
6 · Seen long enoughlast_seen − first_seen ≥ min_seen_secs, by the chain's clock, not the oracle's.
7 · Under the cap, oncepaid_count < max_paid. The paid flag is set before the transfer, so a second payout is impossible.
Payout, inside the same report: reward goes from the Event account to the attendee's wallet and fee (0.002 SOL) goes to the oracle that sent this report, covering what it spends on transactions and deposits. The program also emits AttendeePaid. Until the rules are met, a report only updates the Sighting and nothing moves. Reports after the payout do nothing, so retrying is always safe.
6

Everyone sees it

stage, attendee, explorer
  1. off chain
    The oracle pushes a payout message to the stage, with the wallet label and the transaction. The stage shows it with a Solana Explorer link (devnet).
  2. read
    Anyone can check it: the transaction, the Sighting account (paid flag, times, which oracles reported), and the Event's paid_count and remaining balance.
7

After the event ends

automatic and optional cleanup
  1. off chain
    Each oracle destroys the event's key and its guest list: embeddings and join proofs. After that the faces can't be recovered.
  2. SOL moves
    withdraw_remaining returns the unspent budget to the organizer (also allowed before the start, to cancel). It isn't allowed during the event.
  3. SOL moves
    close_sighting lets the oracle that paid a Sighting's rent close it and get the rent back.
Trust

What an oracle can and cannot do

An oracle that wants to cheat…What stops it
pays someone directly, or pays moreThere is no instruction that pays on request. Only the program moves SOL, with the event's frozen reward and cap.
reports a wallet that never joined (its own, a friend's)It would need that wallet's signature over a join for this event. It can't forge one, and the program checks it on chain.
reports someone who joined but didn't comeWith a threshold of 2 or 3, the other independent oracles must report them too (M-of-N). The organizer chooses the oracles and the threshold.
reports a short visit as a long oneDwell time is measured on the chain clock between reports, not taken from the oracle.
reports outside the event, or for someone already paidThe time window and the per-wallet paid flag are enforced by the program.
reports for an event that didn't choose itThe signer must be in the event's oracle list (NotOracle).
Known limits. A faked camera feed (a photo held up) is handled by liveness and multiple cameras in production, not in this demo. With threshold 1, a single oracle can still report a registered no-show.
